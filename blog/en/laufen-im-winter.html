Cold, dark and still running: how to love winter training

Dark by five, freezing outside, and the sofa has never looked more inviting. And yet a run on a crisp winter morning – frost on the fields, your breath hanging in the air – is one of the best runs of the year. With a few simple tricks, winter running becomes something to look forward to. And come spring, you'll be one step ahead of everyone else.

Dress like an onion

The secret isn't one thick jacket – it's layers. Three thin ones keep you warmer than one bulky one, and you can adjust them as you go:

  • Base layer: a technical top that wicks sweat away from your skin. Cotton stays wet and leaves you chilled.
  • Mid layer: a light long-sleeve or thin fleece – for the really cold days.
  • Outer layer: a jacket to keep out wind and drizzle.

A handy rule of thumb: you should feel slightly chilly when you set off. Ten minutes in, you'll be warm – and if you start out bundled up, you'll be soaked in sweat before long.

Your head, hands and neck lose heat fast. A hat or headband, thin gloves and a neck gaiter are the best winter investments you can make. On frosty days, pull the gaiter up over your mouth and nose to take the edge off the cold air.

See and be seen

In winter, a lot of your running happens in the dark. That can actually be magical – as long as others can see you and you can see where you're going:

  • Go bright and reflective: a vest or jacket with reflective panels, plus reflective bands around your ankles – drivers pick up the movement especially well.
  • Pack a head torch: it shows you roots, puddles and kerbs before you trip over them.
  • Choose lit routes: parks with street lights, riverside paths in town – or a running group that heads out together.

When it gets slippery

On snow and ice, shorten your stride a little and land with your foot flat and right under your body – the same way you'd walk across an icy car park. Trail shoes with a chunky tread grip far better than road shoes, and for icy paths you can get slip-on spikes.

And let's be honest: on black-ice days, a rest day, a treadmill run or a session on the turbo trainer is the smarter call. Your fitness isn't going anywhere.

How to keep going

  • Make it a date: schedule your runs like appointments. Once you know Tuesday at 6 pm is running time, there's nothing left to debate.
  • Run with others: when someone's waiting for you, you show up – drizzle or not. Plenty of clubs and running groups keep going all winter.
  • Grab the daylight: if you can, run at lunchtime. A short run in the midday sun lifts your mood for the rest of the day.
  • Put a race in the diary: a New Year's Eve run, a cross-country race or a winter series gives your training purpose. Check out the list below.
  • Reward yourself: a hot shower, a cup of tea, that glow afterwards. You got out there – while everyone else stayed on the sofa.

Easy is the new fast

Winter is the perfect time for calm, steady miles. There's no need to chase personal bests right now. Lots of easy kilometres build the base that makes you fast in spring. Craving some speed? Throw in a few short strides at the end of a run, once you're properly warmed up.

In the cold, give yourself a longer warm-up and keep the first few minutes deliberately easy. And once you're done: get out of those wet clothes before you cool down.

Indoors counts too

On days when going out just isn't an option, switching to something else isn't giving up: hit the treadmill, ride the turbo trainer, go for a swim or do a few exercises for your core and legs. Strength work in particular pays off once the season gets going again.

The bottom line

Good layers, a light, a running group or a race on the calendar – that's really all it takes. Keep going through the winter and you'll head into spring with a head start. Plus, the best running photos of the year are always taken on frosty mornings.
