Running in winter: how to enjoy training in the cold and dark

It gets dark early, it's cold outside, and the sofa is so cosy. And yet: a run on a clear winter morning, with frost on the meadows and your breath steaming, is one of the best runs of the year. With a few tricks, winter becomes real fun – and in spring you'll be one step ahead of the competition.

Dress like an onion

The secret is layers instead of one thick jacket. Three thin layers keep you warmer than one thick one and can be adjusted on the go:

  • Inside: a technical shirt that wicks sweat away. Cotton stays wet and gets cold.
  • Middle: a light long-sleeved top or a thin fleece – when it's really cold.
  • Outside: a jacket against wind and drizzle.

A good rule of thumb: you should feel a little chilly when you set off. After ten minutes you'll be warm – and if you start out too wrapped up, you'll end up soaked in sweat.

Your head, hands and neck lose heat quickly. A hat or headband, thin gloves and a neck tube are the best investments for winter. In frosty weather you can pull the tube over your mouth and nose, so the air you breathe in is a little warmer.

See and be seen

In winter you often run in the dark. That can actually be really lovely – as long as you are easy to see and can see well yourself:

  • Bright and reflective: a vest or jacket with reflectors, plus reflective bands on your ankles – drivers notice the movement in particular.
  • A head torch shows you roots, puddles and kerbs before you trip over them.
  • Choose lit routes: parks with street lamps, riverside paths in town or a running group that heads out together.

When it gets slippery

On snow and ice it helps to shorten your stride a little and land with your foot flat beneath your body – just like walking on a slippery surface. Trail shoes with a chunky tread grip better than road shoes, and for icy paths there are slip-on spikes.

And honestly: on days with black ice, a rest day, a treadmill run or a session on the turbo trainer is the smarter choice. Your training isn't going anywhere.

How to stick with it

  • Fixed appointments: plan your runs like dates. If you know you run on Tuesday at 6 pm, you don't have to argue with yourself every evening.
  • Run in a group: when someone is waiting for you, you go out even in drizzle. Many clubs and running groups run all winter long.
  • Use your lunch break: if you can, run in daylight – a short run in the midday sun lifts your mood for the whole day.
  • A goal in your calendar: a New Year's Eve run, a cross-country race or a winter running series gives your training a purpose. Our tip: take a look at the list below.
  • Reward yourself: a hot shower, a cup of tea, that great feeling afterwards. You were outside – while others were on the sofa.

Easy is the new fast

Winter is the perfect time for calm, steady runs. You don't need to chase personal bests now. Lots of easy kilometres build the foundation that will make you fast in spring. If you do want some speed: short strides at the end of a run, once you're properly warm.

In the cold, warm up a little longer and take the first few minutes deliberately easy. And after your run: get out of your wet clothes quickly, before you cool down.

Indoors works too

On days when going outside just isn't possible, alternatives aren't giving up: the treadmill, cycling on the turbo trainer, swimming or a few exercises for your core and legs. Strength work in particular pays off when the season starts again.

Conclusion

Good layers, a light, a running group or a fixed goal – that's all it takes. If you keep going through winter, you start spring with a head start. And the best photos of the year are taken on frosty mornings anyway.
