Cross-country and winter running series: racing fun in the cold season

When the days get short, the best racing season is only just beginning for many runners: cross-country races over meadows and forest trails, and winter running series with several dates through to March. Between November and March our list has around 90 cross-country races and more than 130 winter series dates. Here's what to expect – and where you can race.

Cross-country: back to the roots

A cross-country race takes you off the road over grass, forest ground, small hills and sometimes through a whole lot of mud. The courses are usually short – often 5 to 10 kilometres, frequently as a loop you run several times. That makes cross-country ideal for winter: you're never out for long, spectators see you pass several times, and the atmosphere is friendly and familiar.

The best part: on a cross-country course the clock counts for less than usual. Every lap is different, every climb costs energy, and at the end everyone is equally muddy. Many runners find it liberating not to stare at their pace. Along the way you train strength, balance and running form – exactly what makes you faster on the road in spring. If you want to support that specifically, you'll find suitable exercises in “Strength training for runners”.

Winter running series: staying on track with a system

A winter running series is a set of races at the same place, usually every two to four weeks. Often the distances get longer from date to date – a built-in training plan that carries many runners through to their first half marathon in spring. Finish every race and you're in the overall standings, and just having those dates in your calendar helps keep you off the sofa in winter.

In our list every series is one event: expand it and you'll see each date with its own distance.

How to get through mud and cold

  • Shoes with grip: trail shoes grip much better on wet grass than road shoes. You don't need spikes to start with.
  • Lace up tightly: in deep ground the mud loves to tug at your shoes. A double knot saves you from searching for one.
  • Better too light than too warm: it's fine to feel a little chilly at the start. A hat and gloves are still a good idea – you can take them off on the way.
  • Warm up well: in the cold your muscles need longer. A few minutes of easy jogging make the start much more pleasant.
  • Pace by feel: on hills and in mud the pace on your watch doesn't help much. Run by breathing and feel.
  • Dry clothes for afterwards: a change of clothes and a bag for your muddy shoes belong in every cross-country kit bag.

Even more tips for the cold season are in “Running in winter”.

Tip: many cross-country races and winter series are small club events, often with late entry on the day. Still, check the organiser's website beforehand, as some series have a participant limit. And bring your running group: mud is twice as much fun together.

How to find your race

  1. Open the list of all cross-country races or all winter runs.
  2. Set your home town under “Place & radius” – that way you can see straight away what's near you.
  3. Or use the map to find the race with the nicest course.

Conclusion

Winter isn't a break from racing. A cross-country race brings variety and fun to the dark season, and a winter running series gives you dates that keep you on track. Pick a race near you and line up at the start. In spring you'll notice how much good the winter has done you.
