The hard work is done. Race day is around the corner. And now you're supposed to train less? Exactly. Those last one or two weeks are when your body finally cashes in on all the training you've put in. Here's what's going on – and how to nail it.

Tapering in a nutshell

Tapering simply means winding down your training in the final days or weeks before a big race. The goal: shake off the fatigue of your build-up and arrive on the start line fresh, sharp and itching to go[1].

The logic is beautifully simple. Training makes you fitter – but it also makes you tired. And tiredness fades faster than fitness. Ease off just before race day, and the fatigue disappears while the fitness stays[2].

Does it actually work?

It does – and few things in endurance sport are better researched. Large reviews pooling dozens of studies all land in the same place: after a well-planned taper, athletes race faster[3][4].

And it's not just for the pros. Researchers crunched the training data of more than 158,000 recreational marathon runners. Those who cut back week by week over their final three weeks finished, on average, a good five and a half minutes faster than those who kept training hard almost to the end[5].

What your body does while you rest

  • It fills the tank: your muscles top up their glycogen stores – your most important fuel for long distances[2]. Plenty of carbs in the days before the race help[7].
  • It builds more red blood cells: more carriers to ship oxygen to your muscles[2].
  • It rebuilds your muscles: the fibres recover and come back stronger and faster. In swimmers, scientists could even measure this in individual fibres after a three-week taper[6].
  • It lifts your mood: many athletes sleep better, feel better – and the same pace suddenly feels easier[2].

You don't just get fitter. You feel fitter.

How to taper like a pro

What?How?
Volumeroughly halve it
Pacekeep it – a few short, quick efforts stay in
Running dayskeep them – shorten runs instead of skipping them
Lengthabout two weeks, up to three before a marathon
Approachstep it down gradually, not overnight

These guidelines sum up the big research reviews[3][4].

If you remember just one thing: cut the miles, not the speed. Shuffle through your taper at an easy jog and you'll often feel flat on race day. A few brisk efforts keep your legs snappy[1].

What it looks like in practice

Say your biggest week was 50 kilometres over four runs:

  • Two weeks out: around 35 kilometres, still over four runs. The long run gets shorter, but a few kilometres at race pace stay in.
  • Race week: around 20 to 25 kilometres – short, easy runs with a handful of strides.
  • The last day or two: very easy or completely off – whatever feels right.

A 10K needs just a few days of tapering. For a marathon, give yourself longer.

Taper madness is real – and totally normal

Heavy legs, restless evenings, that nagging voice asking whether you're losing your fitness – almost everyone goes through it in taper week. Relax: you're not. Put the extra time to good use instead. Get to bed earlier, plan your trip, lay out your race kit, study the course. Then let the excitement build.

The bottom line

Tapering isn't slacking off. It's the final piece of your training puzzle. Fewer kilometres, the same rhythm, a touch of speed – and you'll toe the line rested, fully fuelled and ready to fly.
