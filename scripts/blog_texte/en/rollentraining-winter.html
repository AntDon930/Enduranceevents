It's wet, it's cold and it's dark by five – but your bike doesn't have to go into hibernation. A turbo trainer in the living room keeps you fit all winter, so you roll into your first spring rides and events with legs that are ready. Here's which trainer suits you, how to set up your corner and what a week of training can look like.

Why the turbo is worth it

Every minute counts indoors. No traffic lights, no freewheeling downhill, no waiting at junctions – you pedal the whole time. That's why an hour inside often feels like an hour and a half outside. On top of that, the weather doesn't matter, you don't need lights, and the shower is a few steps away.

The turbo won't fully replace riding outdoors. But it means you won't be starting from scratch when the days get longer again.

Which trainer is right for you?

TypeWhat to know
RollersThree drums with the bike standing free on top. You have to keep your balance – great for technique, but it takes some practice.
Wheel-on trainerThe rear wheel is clamped in and presses on a roller. Cheap and simple, but louder, and it wears your tyre.
Direct drive (smart trainer)The rear wheel comes off and the chain runs on a cassette on the unit. Quiet and smooth, it sets the resistance itself and connects to training apps.

A simple wheel-on trainer is plenty to get started. If you plan to spend lots of hours indoors or like training with apps, a direct-drive unit is more fun. Second-hand trainers are often cheap – plenty of people sell theirs in spring.

Your winter corner

A few small things make the difference between "this is torture" and "I actually like getting on":

  • A fan. The single most important tip. Without a breeze you overheat quickly and sweat twice as much. A standing fan in front of the bike makes every session nicer.
  • A towel over the bars and a mat under the bike – sweat isn't good for your frame, headset or floor in the long run.
  • Two full bottles. You drink more indoors than out.
  • A mat also cuts the noise if neighbours or family are listening.
  • Entertainment: a series, music, a podcast or a training app. On easy days it's fine for your mind to wander.

Shorter, but often

You don't need to recreate four-hour rides on the turbo. 45 to 60 minutes is enough for most sessions, with something longer at the weekend. Consistency matters more than length: two or three sessions a week all winter beat one week of enthusiasm followed by four weeks off.

Most of your riding should be easy – a pace where you could still chat. Once a week, you can push a little harder.

What a week can look like

DaySession
TuesdayTurbo, 45 minutes easy – steady pedalling, keep an eye on your cadence
ThursdayTurbo, one hour with short efforts, e.g. 5 × 4 minutes brisk with 3 minutes easy in between
Saturday or SundayRide outside if the weather's good, otherwise 75 to 90 minutes easy on the turbo

A run or some strength work on another day fits in nicely. If you do triathlon, you'll find a plan for all three sports in “Swim, spin, run: your triathlon winter plan”.

Keeping it fun

  • Play with cadence: spin faster for a few minutes, then settle back down. It smooths out your pedal stroke.
  • Single-leg drills: short spells pedalling with one leg (rest the other on a stool) – good for an even stroke.
  • Virtual rides: training apps let you ride through landscapes or with other people. A great distraction and a real motivator.
  • Ride together: lots of cycling groups keep going through winter or meet for weekend rides.

Get outside whenever you can

A dry, sunny winter day is a gift. That's the time for a ride outdoors – with the right clothes, lights and a little patience on wet roads. What to wear on the bike in winter is in “Dressed for the cold”. And if you prefer the woods: on a gravel or mountain bike, forest tracks are often nicer than the road in winter.

A goal for spring

With a date in the calendar, getting on the turbo is much easier. Pick a sportive, a long-distance ride or an amateur race for spring now. Which format suits you is explained in “Ride or race?”.

The bottom line

A fan, two bottles and two or three short sessions a week – that's all it takes to get through winter on the turbo. Keep most of it easy, ride outside on nice days and set yourself a goal for spring. Then your first long ride in March will feel great.
