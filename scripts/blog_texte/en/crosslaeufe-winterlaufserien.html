When the days get shorter, the best racing season is just getting started for a lot of runners: cross-country races over fields and forest trails, and winter series with race after race all the way to March. From November to March, our list has around 90 cross-country races and more than 130 winter series dates. Here's what you're in for – and where you can race.

Cross-country: running at its rawest

Cross-country takes you off the tarmac and onto grass, forest floor, short sharp hills – and, quite often, a serious amount of mud. Courses are usually short, typically 5 to 10 kilometres, and often a loop you run several times. That makes it perfect for winter: you're never out for long, spectators see you come past again and again, and the vibe is friendly and down to earth.

The best bit? On a cross-country course, the clock matters less than usual. Every lap is different, every climb bites, and at the end everyone's equally filthy. Plenty of runners find it liberating to stop staring at their pace. Along the way you're building strength, balance and running form – exactly what makes you faster on the road come spring. Winter series: your training plan, race days included

A winter series is a run of races at the same venue, usually every two to four weeks. The distances often grow from one race to the next – a ready-made training plan that carries many runners straight into their first spring half marathon. Finish them all and you're in the overall standings. And just having those dates on the calendar makes it much easier to get off the sofa in winter.

Mud and cold? Here's how to handle them

  • Get some grip: trail shoes hold on wet grass far better than road shoes. You don't need spikes to start with.
  • Lace up tight: deep mud loves to suck the shoes right off your feet. A double knot saves you a frantic search.
  • Underdress rather than overdress: it's fine to feel a bit chilly at the start. A hat and gloves are still a good idea – you can always pull them off mid-race.
  • Warm up properly: cold muscles take longer to get going. A few minutes of easy jogging make the first lap far more pleasant.
  • Run by feel: on hills and in mud, your watch's pace reading is pretty much useless. Go by breathing and effort instead.
  • Pack dry clothes: a change of clothes and a bag for your muddy shoes belong in every cross-country kit bag.

More tips for the cold months in “Winter running”.

Tip: many cross-country races and winter series are small club events, and you can often still enter on the day. Check the organiser's website first, though – some series have a cap on entries. And bring your running crew: mud is twice the fun together.

The bottom line

Winter is no off-season. A cross-country race brings fun and variety to the darkest months, and a winter series gives you dates that keep you honest. Pick a race near you and get on that start line. Come spring, you'll feel just how much good the winter did you.
